# None of Us Is As Dumb As All of Us: Community And Knowledge Go Hand In Hand

**Benjamin Dumke-von der Ehe (Senior Software Engineer — Stacks Labs), Jeff Atwood (Community Wizard)**

`Track: Strategy & Innovation` · `Type: Keynote/Talk` · `Stage: Mainstage`

[Watch the recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GUjcqTMlSug) · [Session page](https://app.wearedevelopers.com/events/16/session/939)

## Abstract

Ben and Jeff met in 2010, when Jeff hired Ben straight out of the nascent community forming on Stack Overflow – but to Jeff's surprise, Ben was not actually a professional software developer (yet). He was a curious tinkerer, a hobby programmer who loved figuring out how things worked, building things, and learning from others. But what is left of the community that helped Ben become successful? How do the curious tinkerers of today form communities to learn what's beneath the surface of the simple LLM answers? We must continue to build a public commons of programming knowledge that benefits everyone, especially in a time when LLM providers dream of monopolizing that knowledge.

## Speakers

### Benjamin Dumke-von der Ehe

*Senior Software Engineer — Stacks Labs*

Ben is an AI-skeptic software developer in Berlin who believes in a healthy balance between over-engineered creativity and creative over-engineering. He was an early hire at Stack Overflow and spent over 12 years there, in addition to working at other startups like Alloy, Wonder, and most recently Stacks Labs.

### Jeff Atwood

*Community Wizard*

Jeff Atwood has spent his career building places where developers learn together at scale. He co-founded Stack Overflow and the Stack Exchange network, transforming Q&A into a global, searchable knowledge base that reshaped how programmers solve problems. He later co-founded Discourse, an open-source discussion platform used by companies, open-source projects, and public communities to host high-signal conversations on the modern web.

Jeff is also the author of the influential Coding Horror blog, known for pragmatic (and occasionally contrarian) perspectives on software engineering, product decisions, and the incentives that shape human behaviour online. His work sits at the intersection of technology and community: how you design systems that are not only technically scalable, but socially sustainable, especially as AI changes how we search, learn, and collaborate.
